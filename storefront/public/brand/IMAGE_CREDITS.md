## What was done to `hero-food-drink.jpg`

The generated file's background was a flat `#EDEEE6` (237, 238, 230) — measured
identical at all four corners and both mid-edges, but slightly darker and
warmer than the page's `#F3F5F0`, so it would have shown as a faint rectangle.

Rather than disguise that at runtime with a blend mode and filters, the
correction is **baked into the file**:

1. **Sparkle removed.** The generator added a small decorative star in the
   lower right. Only pixels *brighter* than the ground were painted out, so the
   wooden board and its contact shadow — both darker — were untouched.
2. **Per-channel gain of `1.0253 / 1.0294 / 1.0435`** maps the ground exactly
   onto `#F3F5F0`. Verified afterwards: all four corners and the right mid-edge
   read exactly `243, 245, 240`.
3. **Outer 22px feathered to the ground.** Left, top and bottom measured clean
   already, so this is a no-op there. It exists for the right edge, where the
   board's shadow grazed the frame and would otherwise have ended in a straight
   vertical line — the defect that spoiled the first attempt. Verified
   afterwards: **zero** pixels on the entire border ring deviate from the
   ground.

**Consequence for the component:** the hero needs no `mix-blend-mode`, no
filter, no mask and no synthetic shadow. Keep it that way — match any
replacement image's background to the section colour instead of reaching for a
blend mode, which costs product colour and erases the photo's real shadow.

## Two earlier attempts, and why they failed

Recorded so the same ground isn't re-covered:

- **Attempt 1** (portrait, wine only) — composition was a narrow vertical stack
  and the contact shadow ran off the **left** edge, terminating in a straight
  line. A shadow already clipped in the source cannot be repaired in CSS.
- **Attempt 2** (asked for a transparent background) — the generator returned
  `colorType=2`, RGB with **no alpha channel**: it had *painted a picture of* a
  transparency checkerboard rather than producing real transparency. Because
  the checkerboard is baked into the pixels, wherever it sat behind the wine
  glass, the steam or the shadow it is blended into them and cannot be
  separated cleanly — stripping it left fragments in exactly those places.
  **Do not ask a generator for a transparent background for this.** Ask for a
  solid one and normalise it, which is what the current file does.


## `campaigns/weekend-collection.jpg`

The editorial banner at the top of the Featured Collection. Paul generated it
from a brief written after the previous image was rejected, and the two
failures that brief was written against are worth keeping:

- **The old image was a scene, not a still life** — a restaurant table with
  three people, glassware and a back wall, all competing. At phone size it
  read as a thumbnail of a crowd.
- **It had no empty area.** The headline and CTA sit over this photograph, so
  copy landed on people's faces.

So the brief asked for: one subject group off to one side, **at least 40% of
the frame empty**, a plain dark background with no visible room, a single
directional light, warm restrained colour, and **nothing readable in frame**.

**Two rules when replacing it.**

1. **Keep the empty area on the same side as the copy.** The copy block is
   bottom-left. An image composed the other way round puts the headline on the
   bottles.
2. **No legible third-party branding, and no legible labels at all.** A
   candidate was rejected earlier for exactly this — a restaurant's name on the
   back wall and a producer's label facing camera. Anything readable reads as
   an endorsement of a business LiquorCentral does not represent. This is also
   why the brief asks for *unlabelled* bottles: a generator inventing a
   real-looking label is the same problem.

### The sparkle, and why it was removed differently from the hero's

The generator added a decorative four-point star in the lower right, the same
artefact `hero-food-drink.jpg` had. It could **not** be removed the same way.
The hero's sparkle sat on flat studio background, so painting out pixels
*brighter than the ground* was safe. This one sits on the clear bottle, over a
bright horizontal glass reflection — a brightness rule would have taken the
reflection with it.

What made it tractable is that everything the sparkle covered is
**horizontally banded**: the liquid line, the base ring and the shadow under
the glass all run left to right. Each row inside the patch was rebuilt by
interpolating between the real pixels just outside it on that same row, which
reconstructs those bands and cannot invent vertical structure that was not
there. The patch is 52×54px in a 1376×768 frame.

## A note on product photography, and what was deliberately *not* committed

The Featured Collection screenshots shown to Paul used **temporary demo
imagery**: three CC-licensed lifestyle photographs assigned to real catalog
products through the Admin API purely so the layout could be judged, then
reverted, with the files deleted. Nothing about that is in this repository.

They were not kept, and should not be, because they are not photographs of
those products — a wine-bar interior is not a bottle of Dom Pérignon.
`BRAND_IDENTITY.md` §15 requires photography to be honest ("what is
photographed is what arrives"), and stand-in imagery on a product card is the
bait-and-switch that rules out, however good it looks in a screenshot.

Real lifestyle product photography remains `BRAND_GUIDELINES.md`'s open item.
Until it exists, product cards render the placeholder frame, which is honest.

## `pairings/*.jpg`

The two slides of the Perfect Pairing banner. Both photographs place their
subjects on the **left** and leave the right half clean — that is not
incidental, it is what the layout depends on: from `small:` up the copy panel
sits over the right half of the image. A replacement composed the other way
round will put text on the food.

Each slide also declares a `theme` (`dark` / `light`) in `pairings.ts`, which
decides every text colour in the panel. Get it wrong on a new image and the
copy is unreadable, not merely off-brand.

**These dishes are illustrative, not photographs of the exact plates that
arrive.** `BRAND_IDENTITY.md` §15 requires honest photography, so if the
kitchen's asun or tilapia looks materially different from these, they need
replacing before launch — the same open item as the rest of the product
photography.

## `products/*.webp` — the bottle cut-outs

Eighteen studio renders Paul generated, committed as **transparent cut-outs**.

**Why transparent and not as supplied.** They arrived on a plain light ground.
A white-ground photograph on the product card's `ink-100` tile shows up as a
white rectangle — the tile tint only does its job if the bottle floats on it.

**How the background was removed, and why it matters.** By flood-filling
inward from the border, *not* by a global lightness threshold. Every one of
these bottles carries a cream or white label, and a global threshold punches
holes straight through them. Only background connected to the edge is removed
— which is also why the cognac case's white satin lining survives: it is
enclosed, not edge-connected.

Each is then trimmed and scaled so the subject occupies the same 70% of a
900px square canvas, so no bottle renders larger than another, and saved as
WebP with alpha. 4.49 MB of PNG became 0.38 MB for the first ten; six more
added since cost another 0.19 MB.

**One addition in the later batch: a speck filter.** The cider shot carried a
decorative four-point sparkle in a corner — the same artefact the hero image
had. Flood fill keeps it, because it is not background, and it then drags the
trim box out with it, shrinking the bottle to make room for a decoration. It
cannot be fixed by keeping only the largest component either, because two of
these renders are a bottle *and* a separate box. So components smaller than
0.5% of the largest are dropped: the sparkle is ~0.02%, the gin's box is 60%.

**⚠️ The brands are invented and do not match the product names.** "High
Coast", "Cristal", "Wildwood", "Caribeño's", "Château Bordeaux", "Glen
Glassaugh", "Obsidian", "El Tesoro", "Maison d'Or", "Domaine du Soleil",
"Crown & Key", "Val de Paix", "Barnerine", "Alta Agave", "Mezcal Artesanal
Oaxaqueño", "Premium Spiced Gold Rum" — none correspond to the catalog's
Macallan, Grey Goose, Bombay Sapphire, Bacardi, Château Margaux, Heineken,
Patrón, Hennessy, Tanqueray, Baileys, Don Julio or Captain Morgan. See
`src/lib/util/demo-product-images.ts` for the full note and the recommended
fix (rename the demo products to the invented brands, which also removes real
trademarks this business does not represent).

Two files are currently referenced by nothing, kept for the first product of
their kind rather than deleted: `wine-white-sauvignon.webp` (the rosé took
`pink-moscato`, the only pink-or-white wine in the catalog) and
`tequila-reposado.webp` (both agave products are *blanco*, so both carry a

**One card still shows a photograph with its studio background**, which is the

`sparkling-prosecco.webp` covers both champagnes. It is a prosecco, which is a
